Hello, I'm Mohsin,
an early-career researcher.

Cancer is most dangerous not where it starts, but where it spreads. I study how, after escaping from the original tumor, cancer cells manage to colonize new tissues despite an adverse microenvironment with low oxygen and altered nutrients.

ORCID Google Scholar GitHub LinkedIn Twitter Facebook Instagram

Bone is one of cancer's most common destinations. My research journey began with bone itself. During my Ph.D. at the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences, I studied how DNA damage and excess mitochondrial reactive oxygen species in bone-forming cells contribute to bone loss. Now, as a postdoctoral researcher, I'm exploring how the stresses cancer cells face in bone shape communication between their mitochondria and nucleus—and how that communication helps them survive and grow there.

I hike, I love cricket, and I rarely say no to a good movie. I also enjoy sharing science with people outside the field, and I can lose hours to history, geography, and what's happening around the world.
